#Solution #unvalidated #evidence/assertion

**The mechanism: start from something valid.** The surface hands back a working skeleton in its accepted dialect — the required preamble, one example of each construct, and the shape a complete artifact takes. The composer edits rather than originates, so the dialect is established by the starting point instead of by anyone's recall.

**Why this shape.** It is the only candidate that works on a composer who neither reads the grammar nor checks midway, because it requires no act at all beyond the one already being performed. Habit is the observed failure mode — TypeScript syntax written confidently into a JavaScript-only file, twice — and a habit that is editing a JavaScript skeleton produces JavaScript.

**Compared to its siblings.** Cheapest to build after the published grammar, and far more likely to actually land than either. Its limitation is scope: a skeleton constrains the parts it shows and says nothing about the parts it does not, so a composer who extends past the example is back to guessing — which is exactly what a hundred-and-seventy-line artifact does. Incremental validation catches that case and this does not, which suggests they are complements rather than alternatives.

**What would make this the wrong pick.** If skeletons calcify what gets built. A starting template is a strong suggestion about structure as well as dialect, and composers tend to stay near it; buying dialect-correctness with a narrowed range of artifacts is a poor trade if the artifacts are meant to be varied. Also worth noting that a stale skeleton is worse than none — it teaches a dialect the surface has since moved off, confidently, which is the failure this whole opportunity is about pointed the other way.
