#AssumptionTest #unvalidated #evidence/assertion

**Lane: humans-required.**

**Pre-committed threshold:** the operator picks one of the two options below, in one sitting, and the answer counts as `supported` only if they pick the agent-records-it option. Anything else — picking the reporting option, declining to choose, or asking for both — is recorded as `refuted` or `inconclusive` rather than read as encouragement. The bar is deliberately one person and one sitting because there is exactly one operator of this vault; a bar phrased as a count would be a fiction dressed as a measurement.

**The ask.** Put three figures in front of the operator — 53 outstanding asks, 473 tests waiting on a person, and the parent opportunity's sample of seven instrument entries of which zero were actionable — and ask them to choose: (a) an unattended firing records a humans-required verdict itself, accepting that some will be wrong and land on your queue, or (b) the count stays single and undifferentiated until you set the labels yourself.

**Why it is framed as a forced choice.** A bare "would you like the agent to do this?" reliably gets a polite yes. Naming the sibling it competes with — a report that splits the count and adjudicates nothing — makes the answer informative either way, which is the comparative use Torres asks of an assumption test. A refusal here does not kill the parent need; it relocates it to the reporting sibling.

**One thing to listen for beyond the answer.** Whether the operator's objection is to the *writing* or to the *queue growing*. Those point at different siblings, and the distinction will not appear in the choice itself.

**What this cannot settle.** Whether the one-way property actually holds in code. That is the sibling assumption's test, it is answerable mechanically, and this ask should not be spent re-litigating it.

A person outside the building is the measurement here: Only the operator can say whether they are willing to have a machine write to their own to-do queue; no exit code encodes a person's consent to being given more work.
