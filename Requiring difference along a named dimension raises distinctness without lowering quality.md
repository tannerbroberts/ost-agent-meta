[[Does a named-dimension constraint raise distinctness without lowering candidate quality]]

Making distinctness a stated property of the set fixes the variations-on-candidate-one problem. It fails if the constraint forces candidates into shapes nobody would have proposed — trading three similar good ideas for three different weak ones.
