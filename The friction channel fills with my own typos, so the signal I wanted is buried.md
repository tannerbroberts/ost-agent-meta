[[Only friction touching the product's own surface is filed; the rest is counted]]
[[Recurrence across sessions files a record, a single incident does not]]

A whole evidence record was generated by one session whose entire friction content is a single line: `(eval):1: == not found`. That is a shell-quoting slip, corrected seconds later, of no interest to anyone. It arrives in the tree with the same standing and the same claim on attention as a report that the loop stalled for four days.

Read across the channel, the pattern holds: shell quoting mistakes, globs that matched nothing, an edit whose search string had drifted, a workflow script written with type annotations. Mechanically these are all `tool_error`. Substantively almost none of them say anything about whether this product is worth building or how it should behave. An earlier hand-count reached the same conclusion — three items found mechanically, all shell-quoting mistakes, none that a human would call product evidence.

The channel therefore taxes exactly the thing it was built to help. Every pass must read and dismiss its noise, and the volume makes the queue look like a backlog of insight rather than a backlog of typos. Worse, a pass under pressure to empty the queue is under pressure to promote a typo into an opportunity.

**The need:** I want the friction that reveals something about the product separated from the friction that is just me mistyping.

More than one way to address this: classify events by whether they touch the product's own surface rather than any tool, weight recurrence over incidence so a one-off slip never files, let a session mark its own friction as meaningful at the moment it happens, or fold self-corrected errors into a counted summary instead of individual records.
