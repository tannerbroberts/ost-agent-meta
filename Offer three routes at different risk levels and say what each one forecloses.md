#Solution #unvalidated #evidence/assertion

Instead of one recommended path, produce three that differ in kind — the safe route that learns slowly, the direct route that commits early, and the cheap route that tests the riskiest belief first — and for each, state plainly what taking it makes harder to do afterwards. The operator picks, and picks knowing the price of being wrong.

Presenting a single most-efficient path hides the assumption that makes it efficient. Three routes make that assumption an explicit choice, which is the difference between advice and a decision.

**Compared to the alternatives.** This is the only option that surfaces reversibility, which is the thing that actually matters when a route turns out to be wrong. Against dependency ordering it is much less mechanical and needs real judgement per route, so it cannot be produced continuously or cheaply. Against a thinking-trace gauge it addresses what to do rather than whether the operator understood the reasoning.

**What would make this the wrong pick.** Three routes are three times the work to produce and to read, and if two of them are obviously worse the whole thing is a recommendation wearing a costume. It is also the option most likely to be generated by a model with no real basis for the risk levels it assigns — in which case the operator is choosing between three fictions with a confident frame around them.
