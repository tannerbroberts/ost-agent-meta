# Founder theory: the generic kernel and the cost curve of ambition (2026-07-24)

Spoken in session by the founder. Evidence class: **assertion** — founder theory,
recorded verbatim in substance for later distillation. No evidentiary weight until
laddered.

**The claim.** The category (AI-assisted opportunity mapping) is already proven by
market existence; the remaining question is which methodology wins the worldwide
explore-exploit race for "prime way of drawing maps." The thesis: one generic kernel
can wrap the heart of every goal metric — anybody, anywhere, maps from where they are
to where they want to be purely with compute (the smartest available model), with the
ambition of the goal determining the compute cost of both discovery and execution.
Conjecture attached: cost curves may make charting incredibly ambitious goals much
cheaper than intuition suggests.

**Session-context observations recorded alongside (agent's, same rung):**

1. The market scan proves the CATEGORY, not this product's demand — the cold-offer
   test's Decides clause (will outside teams hand over real work?) is untouched by
   competitor existence, and its noes now carry more information, not less.
2. Proposed mechanism for the cost conjecture: charting cost scales with the
   FRONTIER (the one credible layer around the present, plus a spine of nested
   sub-outcomes), not with distance-to-goal. Ambition adds height; height is
   structure; structure is what compute is cheapest at. Discovery ~ O(frontier),
   execution ~ O(distance).
3. The curve bifurcates on what kind of reality the goal touches: machine-readable
   feedback (code, telemetry, metrics) → grounding is also compute-purchasable
   (demonstrated 2026-07-24: two solutions killed by replayed data, zero human
   hours). Human-resident feedback (demand, trust, willingness-to-pay) → grounding
   cost floors at reality-contact regardless of model quality.
4. Implied differentiation vs the scanned competitor field: map-drawing is
   commoditizing; believability accounting under compute-inflated structure is not,
   and no scanned competitor advertises it.
