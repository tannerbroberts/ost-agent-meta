This note is the founder's own account of what the OST-Agent is *for*, where it sits relative to the rest of the world, and what he believes it still lacks. It is recorded verbatim so that later passes can distil from the source rather than from a paraphrase. Three concrete needs are embedded in it and are called out at the bottom, but the framing matters as much as the asks.

---

Since the dawn of life, the organism that can reproduce best has been the one that saturates the environment. It is therefore by definition that the organisms that are around today have BEEN around for the past few billion years preciicely BECAUSE they've been so good at functionally mapping their environment onto the survival requirements the organism has for itself. Is it any wonder then, because of this reflective aspect of an organism's function, that human beings and many other multicellular creatures have developed self-awareness? Not in the slightest! Beings that can not only respond to their environment, but actively integrate the information available to them in new and powerful ways such that their responses become much more neuanced, thrify, and impactful, are the sorts of individuals that tend to have a competitive advantage in the world of survival. In fact, the ability of a system to be able to ingest and digest information is SUCH a useful trait that AI machine learning has taken quite a strong hold in the year 2026. So what is the entity that wins in a conflict today? I say it's one that can assimilate information best in this day and age. I'll argue that it MUST be an entity that thinks at the speed of light, because anything else running the same processes at even half that speed (still incredible) would suffer dire consequences if competing directly with the leader via only being able to work at half speed. What else can we say about this entity? Well one interesting aspect of this entity is that, because people are so hell-bent about doing less work for more reward, this entity is very likely to be born out of people's desire to cut corners whilst still reaping full rewards. Another thing we should note is that this entity, being born out of the human motivation to not need motivation, is MUCH more substrate independent than people, because people are also so very bad at picking one idea and sticking to it, everything they build that lasts, must by necesity, be somthing that can port. Well, of course that thing is AI, nobody's questioned that, but AI still has critical flaws that stop it from being all that people want it to be. For starters, AI can only run inference on the tokenized inputs that are shoved through an API pay wall. It can only do the type of inference that fine-tuning has allowed for or encouraged. It is a terrible stuard of it's own working environment by default due to the fact that it sways with the slightest provocation on it's previous ground truths. It's context dependent, and the context object is SO INFURIATINGLY BAD! But fear not! Because people have discovered that they too fail in this way. People too have discovered that what matters more than their own opinions, are the data that supports their opinions. This is where the beauty of this application comes into full light. AI systems need an excuse to do a thing. That excuse can be as simple as a prompt file. If the task is get better at doing tasks, the loop will likely halt. But if there is a bullish persistence to feed that loop, and push it to build on itself, then maybe that loop CAN be turned over a few times, and increacingly faster as time goes on. Of course, this has already happened. The "AI race", or the "AI bubble", depending on who you talk to, has fed on it's own success. What success? The stories are many and varried, but everybody seems to understand that AI is working, and working better every day. That trend on the large scale is something that plays out on the small scale as well. An AI agent that needs to solve a task in a context window can go out into the world and find it's own evidence, draw it's own conclusions, and make it's own fixes to it's own problems. Fine, but that's too small for this app. This app sits right in the middle of those two worlds. It stiches together the recursive self-improvement that's happening on a global scale, and the recursive self-improvement that's happening in the context window. The information gathered is in terms of pain points, bottlenecks, slowdowns, and particularly, in terms that the AI agent has itself declared are likely to move the core metric of the OST. That data though, it's not just "trust me bro", it's grounded in experiments that are falsifiable, proovably good for the moving of the metric. Here's the real kicker, all three systems are goal-agnostic. The paperclip maximizer, the filling of the context window in real-time with just out of reach facts, and the building out of an OST can all be pointed at an arbitrary human goal.

There are benchmarks for tool use. A model is given tools and asked questions. Retrieval tools, for example, like grep, have been very easy to train, because the rewards are very easy to cook up. If there were a benchmark that this tool aims at, it would be the strategic decomposition of the task of getting the information needed to reliably build the thing requested. It's just a much larger version of the context window, and a much smaller version of what's going on in the world at large.

I think a genuine need of this system is that of usage telemetry, and I want all of it, not just the summaries. Building input channels that can handle such an influx of information, and keep it all straight, that's an engineering task in and of itself, but it's got to be done for the larger goals out there.

That's another thought I just had. There are different levels of OST... There's the top-level goal of "10,000 daily active users", but then there's the much more concrete "decrace landing page dropoff to 5%" or something similar. Building an app that's worthy of 10,000 daily active users is no small feat, and there are ALL sorts of tradeoffs to make between here and there, but that's the job, mapping the most effective route from here to there. Here being the "not goal achieved" state, and there being the "goal achieved" state. It's all information processing, and especially processing the information of the supposed opportunities.

It's been said of ideas that they're really great avatars, because they can die so easily, and by so doing, seed control back to the organism that spawned them. Oh they fight for their lives too, make no mistake, but they die much more easily, and if an idea can be killed on vibes, really, how likely would it be that it succeeds. Well that depends on how attuned the vibes are to reality. The OST-Agent wants to do a similar thing. It wants to spawn all the most likely maps to the goal in memory, and find reasons to kill each of them, grounded in realitie's own truth. The creativity needed to do that is incredible, and I worry that the agent won't have enough creativity to be up to the task, but we'll see.

## The needs embedded above, for whoever distils this

1. **Raw usage telemetry, not summaries.** "I want all of it, not just the summaries." The channel that can absorb that influx and keep it straight is itself the engineering problem.
2. **Levels of OST.** A top-level goal ("10,000 daily active users") and a concrete, movable one ("landing page dropoff to 5%") are not the same altitude, and the job is mapping the most effective route between the not-achieved and achieved states.
3. **Ideas as cheap avatars.** Spawn the likely maps to the goal, then find grounded reasons to *kill* each one. Stated worry: the agent may not be creative enough to spawn maps worth killing.

Framing that is not itself a need, but that the above rests on: the product's claimed position is the seam between the global recursive-improvement loop and the in-context one; its target capability is *strategic decomposition of the information-gathering task*; and all three levels are goal-agnostic — pointable at an arbitrary human goal.
