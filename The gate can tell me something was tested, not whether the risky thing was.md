#Opportunity #unvalidated #evidence/assertion

When a mechanical gate reports a solution as cleared, all it actually knows is that *some* assumption beneath it recorded a result. Whether the assumption that got tested was the one that would kill the idea if it were wrong is a judgement about the world, not about the tree's shape — and nothing in the loop makes that gap visible.

The builder's own report puts it plainly: the floor ("something was tested") is unambiguous and needs no model; the ceiling ("the right thing was tested") is not mechanically judgeable at all. A cleared gate therefore reads as more reassurance than it has earned, and the person relying on it cannot see which half they are getting.

**The need:** I want to know whether the assumption that was tested is the one that could sink the solution — not merely that the count is non-zero.

There is more than one way to address this: rank proposed tests by declared risk before any run, require a solution to name its riskiest assumption and point the gate at that one specifically, report cleared-but-shallow separately from cleared, or have an independent reviewer rate whether the tested assumption was the load-bearing one.
