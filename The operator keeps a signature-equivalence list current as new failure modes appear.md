#Assumption #unvalidated #evidence/assertion
[[Count from git history how many hand-held vault artifacts the operator has amended more than once]]

**The belief, stated so it could be false.** The one person who can judge whether two errors are the same problem will keep doing so, month after month, as new failure modes appear.

This project's own tree is the best evidence against it. Artifacts asking for recurring input from this founder are the ones that have gone unmaintained, and there is a live assumption elsewhere on exactly this question — "The founder will actually maintain a highlight criteria note over time" — which no pass has been able to settle. Asking for a second such artifact before the first one's answer is known is the kind of bet worth naming as a bet.

**Risk category: viability.** Not whether the mechanism works, and not whether anyone wants a shorter queue — whether the ongoing cost gets paid by the only person who can pay it.

**The failure is asymmetric, and in this candidate's favour.** A stale list leaves signatures unmarked, and unmarked signatures stay individually listed. So neglect degrades to today's noisy queue rather than to a buried finding — unlike the age-out sibling, where a wrong rule collapses things nobody inspected. That asymmetry is the strongest thing this candidate has and should be weighed against its higher ongoing cost.

**A cheaper route to the answer than waiting, worth trying first.** The sibling branch "An operator-set evidence window in ost.config.yaml, amended by hand like discovery.target" proposes settling this class of belief by reading amendment counts out of the vault's own git log, rather than by seeding an artifact and waiting weeks to see whether it rots. If the founder has never amended a comparable hand-held artefact, that is evidence about this belief obtained for the price of reading a log. The test beneath this node takes that route.
