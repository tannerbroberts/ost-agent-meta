#Opportunity #unvalidated #evidence/assertion
[[Keep the goal I actually want as the outcome, and hang the affordable one beneath it as a milestone]]

Ambition and cost feel proportional, so the goal that gets charted is the modest one. If mapping a route to somewhere far away is assumed to cost in proportion to the distance, nobody starts the far map — they pick a target sized to what they can already justify spending, and never learn what the ambitious one would have cost.

The founder's conjecture is that the intuition is wrong: charting cost may scale with the frontier — the one credible layer around the present, plus a spine of nested sub-outcomes — rather than with distance to the goal. Ambition adds height, height is structure, and structure is the thing compute is cheapest at. If that holds, discovery cost tracks the frontier's width while execution cost tracks the distance, and the two have been conflated. It is a conjecture, recorded at the rung it deserves; the tree does not yet know whether it is true.

**The need:** I want to know what charting a far-off goal would actually cost me before I decide to aim lower.

More than one way to address this: show the frontier width a goal implies before any pass runs, price a chart from its spine rather than its horizon, let a goal be entered at full ambition and let the tree grow the spine incrementally, or report cost-per-pass against tree height so the curve becomes observable.
