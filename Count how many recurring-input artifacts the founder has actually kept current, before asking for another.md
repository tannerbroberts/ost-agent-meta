#AssumptionTest #unvalidated #evidence/assertion

**Lane: humans-required.**

**The design.** Sit with the founder and list every artifact in this project that asks for their recurring input — the highlight criteria note this tree already carries a live assumption about, the config files that encode policy, any hand-maintained lists. For each: when was it last edited, what change was it supposed to track, and did the edit follow that change or lag it? Then ask directly about the ones that lagged — was it forgotten, was it not worth it, or did the artifact stop mattering?

**Pre-committed bar, stated before looking:** at least 2 of the existing recurring-input artifacts were edited within 30 days of the change they were meant to track. Below that, the manifest candidate should be treated as refuted on viability and the sibling that deletes the repeat inference outright becomes the default, because it asks nobody for anything.

**Why a person and not a spec.** File timestamps are on disk and a script could gather them, but timestamps answer the uninteresting half. The half that decides this is *why* an artifact went stale, and whether a manifest of loop-prescribed call sequences is the kind of thing this particular founder would keep up — which is a claim about one person's habits that only that person can report. Gathering the timestamps first is worth doing so the conversation starts from evidence rather than recollection.

**What this does not settle.** Nothing about whether suppression is the right shape of fix. A founder who maintains the manifest faithfully could be maintaining a mechanism that hides the wrong events — that is the sibling candidates' question. It also cannot tell you what a *different* operator would do, and this tree's standing caution about founder-sourced evidence applies in full: one person's answer is rung `stated` at best and is not demand data.

A person outside the building is the measurement here: Only the founder can say which artifacts they intended to keep current and why they stopped; the interesting half of the answer is the reason an abandoned one was abandoned, which no file timestamp carries. It is a question about one specific person's habits, asked of that person.
