[[An operator consents to being asked, and finds the questions worth their time]]

Invert the usual arrangement. The agent does the work, and then, at a small number of chosen moments, deliberately does not — it presents the two candidate opportunities it was about to choose between and asks the operator which, or shows the solution it was about to rule out and asks whether that is right. It knows its own answer and withholds it until the operator has given theirs.

Understanding is maintained by being made to decide under uncertainty, which is what tending the tree by hand was actually doing. Reading a briefing does not do it; approving a draft barely does. Answering before you have seen the answer does.

**Compared to the alternatives.** Costs the operator very little time and buys a genuine test of whether they still understand their own tree — and if their answers start diverging from the agent's, that divergence is itself the signal this opportunity is about. Neither of the other options produces any such signal. It is also the most intrusive per interaction and depends on the agent choosing good moments, which is a judgement call it may make badly.

**What would make this the wrong pick.** An agent that withholds its answer to test its operator is doing something the operator has to have agreed to, and an operator in a hurry will experience it as being quizzed by their own tooling. Consent and frequency both have to be the human's, not the agent's.
