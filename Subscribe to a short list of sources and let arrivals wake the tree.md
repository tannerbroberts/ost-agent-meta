#Solution #unvalidated #evidence/assertion
[[A chosen source list delivers items that bear on something actually open]]

Rather than searching, watch. The operator names a small set of sources that actually bear on this domain — a few feeds, a changelog, a competitor's release notes, one or two researchers — and the vault polls them cheaply for new items. New material arrives as evidence with the source's earned rung already attached, and the tree is woken by the arrival.

Trust is what makes this affordable. A subscription list is a standing judgement about who is worth reading, so the ranking work happens once per source instead of once per finding, and the ladder already has a place to record it.

**Compared to the alternatives.** Cheapest per finding of the three, since polling a feed costs nothing like a search does, and it is the only one that notices a source changing its mind. Against demand-pulled lookups it is much less targeted — most arrivals will bear on nothing — and against an open research loop it is far narrower, seeing only what the named sources publish.

**What would make this the wrong pick.** The list is chosen by the same person whose blind spots are the problem, and it hardens over time. A subscription set assembled a year ago is a very efficient way to keep reading the wrong people, and nothing in the mechanism will say so.
