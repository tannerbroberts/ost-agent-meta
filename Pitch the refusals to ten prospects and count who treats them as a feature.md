#AssumptionTest #unvalidated #evidence/assertion

The assumption underneath this whole position is that buyers punish overclaiming. They may instead prefer the tool that says yes, and experience the refusals as the product being worse.

**Risk category: desirability.**

**Design.** Describe the tool to ten prospects, leading with what it will not do — cannot mark its own work validated, cannot record a result nobody watched, caps every claim at the rung its source earned. Do not soften it. Then ask what they made of that, in their own words, before asking anything else. Count who volunteers it as a reason to trust the output versus who raises it as a limitation.

**Why it is small.** Ten conversations, no build, and it can be run against the existing description.

**What it will not cover.** What people say about a described product is not what they do with a real one, and the ladder ranks it accordingly. A prospect who admires rigour in conversation may still buy the tool that agrees with them.

A human runs this and records the result.
