[[A decay rule surfaces the nodes that have actually gone out of date]]

Evidence ages. A node whose supporting evidence has not been refreshed loses weight over time and eventually surfaces as "this hasn't been checked in months — is it still true?", so the map reflects the world now rather than the world when it was written.

**How it differs from its siblings:** keeps the map *honest over time* rather than well-organised or well-remembered. It is the only sibling that makes an unchanged node get quieter on its own.

**Trade-off:** decay rate is a guess; some needs are stable for years and constant re-verification prompts train people to ignore them.

**Riskiest assumptions to test:** that a decay rate exists that flags real staleness without crying wolf (feasibility); that stale flags get acted on rather than dismissed (desirability).
