#Opportunity #unvalidated #needs-customer-interview #founder-theory #evidence/assertion
[[Independent ideators that never see each other's candidates]]
[[Forced-variation prompts that require candidates to differ on a named dimension]]
[[Outside-in candidates drawn from how other products solved the same opportunity]]

**The need (customer's voice):** "When I ask for options I get three phrasings of one idea. If the map that would actually get me there isn't in the set, nothing downstream saves me — I'll just carefully, rigorously kill the wrong things and feel good about the process."

**Why it matters:** Elimination is bounded above by generation: a disciplined kill process applied to a narrow candidate set converges precisely and wrongly. This is the founder's own stated worry about the product — "the creativity needed to do that is incredible, and I worry that the agent won't have enough creativity to be up to the task" — and it is the rare case where the person closest to the system named its ceiling rather than its features. The ruleset already prefers generating more solutions where risk or differentiation is high, but nothing anywhere measures whether the generated set is *diverse* or merely numerous.

**Litmus test (is this an opportunity, not a solution?):** Several distinct directions — independent blind ideators, forced variation along named dimensions, candidates drawn from outside the current context, deliberate constraint perturbation, or human ideation injected at the widest point. Passes.

**Relationship to its sibling:** Complement to "Nothing kills a candidate, so every idea I have ever had is still alive" — that one is elimination, this one is generation, and they fail in opposite directions. Recorded as siblings rather than nested because they are distinct alternatives at the same level, but a human may prefer to nest them under a shared parent about the quality of the search; flagged rather than decided.

**Provenance caveat:** Founder-stated in a single spoken rant, not sourced from a story-based customer interview. Believability rests on the floor rung (`assertion`). This is a hypothesis about a need, not an observed need; a human should confirm or discard it against real customer conversations before anything is built off it.
