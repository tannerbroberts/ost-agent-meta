#Assumption #machine-witness #goal-acquisition #unvalidated #evidence/assertion
[[Gate refuses a permit when the claim's author is the sole author of its judge]]

**Risk category: feasibility.**

The belief, stated so it can be wrong: the vault's own records carry enough provenance — which actor authored a solution's claim, which actor authored the test that judges it, which process recorded the verdict — that the gate-parity rule "the party that authored the claim is not the sole author of its judge" can be evaluated mechanically at gate time, with no human reading history.

Ways it fails, named at birth: authorship may not be recorded at the granularity the rule needs (a session id is not an author when one actor drives every session); History lines are prose, and prose provenance may not parse; and an agent that knows the rule can trivially split its work across two sessions, satisfying the letter of separation while remaining one author — in which case the check measures ceremony, not independence. If any of these holds, the gate-parity solution loses the counterparty substitute it promised and quietly becomes self-grading with extra steps.

This is a question about code and records, not about people: whether a guard can read authorship from what the vault already writes is settled by a spec against the gate, in minutes.
