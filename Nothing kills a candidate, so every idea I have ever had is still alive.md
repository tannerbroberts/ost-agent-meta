**The need (customer's voice):** "I can generate maps to the goal all day. What I can't do is get rid of them. Every one sits at 'unvalidated' forever, so the tree grows and my decision gets harder rather than easier. I want reasons to kill these things that come from reality, not from my mood on the day."

**Why it matters:** The founder's framing is that ideas are valuable *because they die cheaply* — "they're really great avatars, because they can die so easily, and by so doing, seed control back to the organism that spawned them." A tree that only accumulates gives up that property: it turns cheap, disposable candidates into permanent obligations. His own qualifier is the crux — an idea killed on vibes is fine *if the vibes are attuned to reality*, which is exactly what a grounded, falsifiable kill is and what an unexamined preference is not. Today this tree carries dozens of solutions, none tested and none dead.

**Litmus test (is this an opportunity, not a solution?):** Many distinct directions — pre-committed kill criteria, ordering tests by how fast they could disconfirm, adversarial red-teaming of candidates, an evidence-debt gate, staleness decay that proposes archival, or head-to-head elimination between candidates. Passes.

**Relationship to existing branches:** Adjacent to "I can't tell which evidence deserves weight" (which concerns how much to *trust* a claim) and to the existing "Evidence-debt gate before building" solution — but distinct: this need is about *removal*, and nothing in the tree currently removes anything. Flagged for a human in case they would rather nest it.

**Provenance caveat:** Founder-stated in a single spoken rant, not sourced from a story-based customer interview. Believability rests on the floor rung (`assertion`). This is a hypothesis about a need, not an observed need; a human should confirm or discard it against real customer conversations before anything is built off it.
